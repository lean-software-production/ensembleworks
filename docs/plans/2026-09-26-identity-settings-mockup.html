Identity settings: three UX directions

Each direction is drawn inside BB's real settings-page chrome as it exists today. BB renders the plugin header and the generated form from bb.settings.define; Identity's settingsSection renders beneath that form. All names, emails, hosts and origins below are invented sample data.

Direction 1 · Setup path

Thesis: most confusion comes from settings whose meaning depends on each other: picker, origin, key and fallback; enforcement and team machines. So the section becomes an ordered list of decisions. Each one shows its live readiness and names what it depends on. The first question asked is how people actually reach the server, and the recommendations follow from the answer. The generated form stays in place as the “advanced” surface.

What Identity is, and is not

Identity labels who started what, and it can stop honest mistakes such as starting a thread on a teammate's machine. It is not access control. Anyone who can reach this server can still use Send-now, a terminal, or the API. Exists today · README trust statement

One exception to the order

If a browser's choice has expired, or names someone no longer in the directory, that browser shows as anonymous. It doesn't fall through to the fallback email, so a stale choice can't become someone else.

Step 5 of 6

Catch honest mistakes, in stages

All three stages make the same decision. They differ only in what happens next.

  1. OffRecords who started what and labels it. Refuses nothing and logs nothing.
  2. Audit Current · since 18 SepLogs every dispatch it would refuse as a would-refuse line in the plugin log, then lets it through.
  3. EnforceRefuses those dispatches with a message that names the rule and suggests a fix.
Rule AStarting on another person's machineTeam and unclaimed machines are always fine.
Rule BSending into a thread someone else startedOther people's threads are read-only.
Rule CAn automation off a team machineOnly covers automations that start a new thread. An automation sent into an existing thread arrives unstamped, and Identity can't see it. BB core: stamp threads.send

Always allowed, in every stage

Anything Identity can't tie to a person: an agent's own bb thread tell, retries, plugin follow-ups, the CLI, and names from the fallback email or a browser choice. Send-now, terminals and raw API calls never pass through the guardrail at all.

New Identity code

IA, journey and first run
  • One section with six ordered steps: Server profile → People → Machines → Browser names → Guardrail → Check. Each step's status line comes from live data, so the rail also works as a dashboard on return visits.
  • First run opens on the profile question and pre-selects what Identity can detect: whether this request carried an Access header. Picking an answer previews changes as a Now → Recommended table, and nothing is written until “Apply”.
  • People and emails stay read-only (Ansible renders them). The People step links to a “copy entry” action and doesn't offer editing.
  • The precedence ladder appears where the confusion happens, next to the picker, with a “you are here” marker.
Audit vs enforcement, diagnostics
  • The rollout is shown as three stages of one decision. This matches the code: audit and enforce call the same decideGuardrail.
  • The pre-Enforce checklist names the people who would be refused (from pin conflicts), not just a count.
  • Would-refuse counts are shown as unavailable and labelled as a BB core and owner decision. The jq command is the supported path today.
  • Check shows self-test legs, the picker chain and config lint, and the failing step turns red on the rail.
Responsive, accessibility, safety
  • Desktop: 220px rail plus the step panel. Narrow sidebar and mobile: the rail becomes a horizontally scrolling stepper above the panel, and each chip keeps its status text.
  • The rail is an ordered list with aria-current="step". Statuses are always icon plus text, never colour alone.
  • Enforce and key rotation open confirm dialogs with the safe button focused. They become bottom sheets on compact viewports, matching BB's Dialog.
Trade-offs and migration
Advantages
  • Best first-run experience. Dependencies are explicit.
  • Staged rollout is the natural home for audit.
  • Recommendations follow from one answer.
Disadvantages
  • Returning admins must walk the steps to find one fact (“who owns this host?”).
  • A wizard can suggest a finished setup when people are managed in infra.
  • Colours and People get squeezed.

Complexity: medium. Mostly reads existing RPCs. Writes need one new Identity RPC over experimental_set. Migration: the People roster moves into step 2, and the section id changes from people to setup.

Direction 2 · People & machines directory

Thesis: admins come back with questions about a specific thing. “Why is Priya's machine showing Erin?” “Which emails count as Alex?” “Why am I anonymous in this browser?” So the section is organised around the objects: people, machines and this browser. Each fact sits on its object with its provenance (where it came from and what it counts for). The rules sit in a fourth tab. An identity bar always shows who you are right now and how Identity knows.

How a machine gets its owner
  1. Listed in Team machines: shown as the team machine.
  2. Pinned owner: the first owner Identity saw, kept when a machine is renamed so a rename can't hand it to someone else.
  3. Name suffix: the part after the last dash matches someone's person name or GitHub login.
  4. Otherwise unclaimed, and never silently shown as the team machine.

Guessing from names is a stand-in. BB core: an owner field on hosts would replace steps 2 and 3. Today there is no way to unpin, and conflicts are reported from the moment Identity loads. Re-pin and unpin need New Identity code.

Direction 3 · Trust & operations console

Thesis: the biggest risk with Identity is overestimating it. Admins switch on Enforce believing it's a lock, or trust a browser-chosen name. So this section leads with what Identity can and can't see. It explains any decision on demand: “why am I shown as…?” and “what would the guardrail do if…?”. Operational health is treated as a first-class screen. People and machines appear as inputs to those explanations and don't get their own screens.

The baseline is what ships today. All three directions are measured against it. The recommendation combines them rather than picking one outright.

What today does well: honest, precise copy. Colours with a contrast floor. The picker, ownership chip and read-only banners already carry the attribution-only message.

What it leaves to the generated form or the CLI: precedence, machine ownership and pins, team machines, picker readiness, self-test results, enforcement consequences, and the directory being managed by Ansible.

Recommendation: Direction 2 as the backbone, plus the best of 1 and 3

  1. Backbone (Direction 2): an object-first People · Machines · This browser · Health section. It grows out of today's People roster (the section id can stay people) and answers the questions admins actually bring back.
  2. From Direction 1: a compact readiness strip above the tabs (Profile · People · Machines · Browser names · Guardrail · Check), each with a live status that links to its tab. The server-profile question shows only on first run, while the directory is empty. The precedence ladder goes in This browser.
  3. From Direction 3: the Health tab (self-test legs, picker chain, ledgers, config lint, redacted diagnostics), the coverage map as a disclosure under Rules, and the guardrail simulator, which reuses the pure decideGuardrail.
  4. Defer: in-UI audit evidence (counts, recent would-refuse). It needs a BB core log-query API and the owner reversing the 18 Sep no-UI-log decision. Ship the copyable jq command meanwhile.

Why not Direction 1 alone? It's best on day one and worst on day thirty. Why not Direction 3 alone? Its best feature is the one that's blocked. The combination keeps Direction 3's honesty everywhere through the identity bar, provenance columns and the “Always allowed” card.
